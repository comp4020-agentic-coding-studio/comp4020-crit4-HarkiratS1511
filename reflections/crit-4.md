# Crit 4 — An instrument

## The breakthrough

Picking the handpan was the easy half. The half that actually moved the work
was realising that porting one faithfully would be worthless. A handpan already
sounds good and is already easy for a stranger to play — if I just rebuilt it in
the browser, the best possible outcome was a worse version of something that
already exists.

What unlocked it was turning the question around: instead of asking what a
handpan does, I listed what a handpan *can't* do, and built those. Steel hides
its sympathetic resonance inside the shell, so I made the fields light up with
the same envelope that drives their gain — the glow isn't a picture of the
sound, it's the sound's own state drawn twice. Steel has no memory, so the pan
listens for a phrase and answers it a second later, quieter, fading out. And
steel is one scale forever, so mine glides between scales while notes are still
ringing.

That reframing is the thing I'd keep. Skeuomorphism isn't embarrassing because
it copies — it's embarrassing when it copies the limitations too.

## What it changed about me

A handpan costs around two thousand dollars and comes in exactly one scale. You
can't try one first. You commit that money and then find out whether D Kurd is
the sound you actually wanted.

I think the web is short of places to try things like that. Not demos or
marketing pages — actual working versions you can put your hands on before
spending money you can't get back. Building this made me want to make more of
them: the kind of site that exists so someone can find out what they want.
